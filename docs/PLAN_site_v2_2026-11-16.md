## The idea in one line
The watch assembles while the call gets made. The viewer sees the inputs arrive, then the decision narrow, and it all lands on "Fastball, outside." Then they change the situation themselves.

## The look

**Color** (all as CSS variables)
| Name | Value | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `#0c0b0e` | Page background. Same as the film's frames, so there is no seam. |
| Midnight | `#14224f` | Large soft light behind sections |
| Read glow | `#2f5bff` | Inputs: dots, bars, lit controls |
| Chalk | `#ecebe6` | All text, including the stop 1 headline |
| Link green | `#62f07a` | The call and what leads to it |

- Blue means "being weighed". Green means "the call".
- **Gradient or green text appears only at the moment a call lands**: the call words at stop 6, and the share next to the winning call. Every other headline is plain chalk.
- Green on things that are not text: the rail as it fills, the winning bar, the glass rim once a call wins, the "Call landed" chip, the light behind the watch, the line into the watch in the playground, and primary buttons.
- Blends are mixed in OKLab from `#2f5bff` to `#62f07a`.

**Glass**
- Blurred and saturated backdrop, a bright top rim, an inner bloom, a blue glow along the bottom that turns green when a call wins, and a highlight that follows the cursor.
- Real refraction at the glass edge is in the prototype and works in Chrome (an SVG displacement map behind the backdrop). Other browsers get the blurred glass. It is subtle: the edge of the watch bends where a chip crosses it.
- Glass is for the nav, the film's ledger and chips, the playground panels and small chips. Text sections sit on the plain ground.

## The film: six stops
The layout holds for stops 1 to 3, then breaks.

| Stop | Film shows | Statement | What moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parts scattered | Every pitch called. Every call explained. (plain chalk) | Nothing yet |
| 2 | Case comes in | It starts with the situation. | Ledger on the right fills: 2-2, one out, runner on third, 3rd inning, tied |
| 3 | Board and screen | Then your pitcher. Then the hitter. | Ledger adds feel for each pitch, same-side hitter, last pitch a slider away |
| 4 | Watch closes up | Every pitch, every spot, weighed. | **The ledger opens up**: the candidate calls leave it and sit loose around the watch as separate glass chips |
| 5 | Screen wakes | Down to one. (set much larger) | **The four losing calls fall away across the screen**; the winner stays, grows and turns green |
| 6 | FASTBALL / OUTSIDE | The call words | **FASTBALL and OUTSIDE fill the width**, one on each side of the watch, in the blue-to-green blend, in the same order as the watch face |

- Stops 1 to 3 keep one layout: statement left, watch middle, ledger right, rail at the bottom. Stop 3's statement needs its own position on desktop because the watch is large and off-center in those frames.
- Stop 6 carries the line that explains the share: "Fastball, outside is 42% of the mix here, not 100%. The engine mixes on purpose, so a hitter can't sit on one pitch."
- Phone: at stop 4 the chips stack under the watch; at stop 6 FASTBALL sits above the watch and OUTSIDE below it, each the full width.
- The story is one real engine run, computed at build time through the same engine the demo uses. If a later engine version changes the top call for this situation, the build fails loudly so the story gets a new true situation.

## Making the words match (Drew's point 4)
The watch app has no call that reads just "OUTSIDE". Its words for the outside spots are LOW AND OUTSIDE and HIGH AND OUTSIDE (`app/watch.js`). So no real situation can make the engine's exact call match the film's "FASTBALL / OUTSIDE" word for word. The options:

- **A. Tell the film's story by side of the plate (in the prototype).** The candidates are grouped by pitch and side: "Fastball, outside" is low-and-outside plus high-and-outside added together, 42% in this run, and it is the top group. The watch, the headline and the chips then all say "Fastball, outside", and all of it is true. The playground shows the exact calls in the watch's own words. Cost: the film simplifies one level; the exact spot shows up only in the playground.
- **B. Re-render the last few film frames** so the watch reads FASTBALL / LOW AND OUTSIDE, and tell the story with the exact call (26%). Most exact. Cost: it breaks "don't re-render the film" and needs Blender time.
- **C. Cover the film's screen with a live face** that says LOW AND OUTSIDE once the film rests. No re-render. Cost: the words on the watch visibly change at the end of the film.

Recommendation: A now, and B whenever the film is re-rendered for another reason (the build report already lists a higher-sample re-render as optional).

Also true in every option: the real watch shows the catcher's sign number, not the pitch name. The page already labels the watch as an illustration.

## Set the situation (the playground)
- Left glass panel: the count (large numerals with ball and strike steppers), tap-to-toggle runners, outs, and the inning in a tied game.
- Right glass panel: what the engine would throw here, as bars that re-sort when anything changes, with the engine's own reason line under them.
- The watch is the film's own last frame. When the situation changes, a line runs from the mix into the watch (blue to green), the light behind the watch goes green, and a live face on the screen redraws with the new call in the watch's words. "Get it over" shows the pitch alone, as the real watch does.
- It runs the real engine in the browser. The watch shows the most likely call; the fine print says that in a game the engine draws from the mix.
- A hitter control ships only if it can drive a real engine input. In the prototype the hitter is fixed (same side, just saw a slider low and outside) and says so.

## The at-bat (new plan, not a restyle)
**Idea:** one at-bat as a strip of watch faces. The film showed one call; this shows how each call depends on the last.
- **Layout:** a pinned row of four or five watch faces, one per pitch, each with its result under it (ball, foul, swing and miss). Above the row, one line of copy that changes per pitch and says what the last pitch set up, in the engine's own reason words. Data comes from the real engine output the current at-bat section already uses.
- **Motion moment: the thread.** As you scroll, a blue line leaves the face that was just thrown, picks up that pitch's speed and height, and runs into the next face, which then lights green as its call lands. On the last pitch the whole thread lights at once, so the at-bat reads as one plan.
- **Interaction:** hover or tap any face to replay how its call was reached (the mix for that pitch opens in a glass chip).
- **Phone:** the row becomes a vertical stack that scrolls normally, with the thread running down the left edge.
- **Reduced motion:** the faces and reasons as a plain list.
- The current 3D field scene is dropped from this section. It is the heaviest thing on the page (3.7 MB of frames plus three.js) and the faces tell the sequencing story more directly. Say if you want the field kept.

## Two wrists (new plan)
**Idea:** the pitcher and the catcher get the same call at the same moment.
- **Layout:** the one watch from the film splits into two, side by side, labelled Pitcher and Catcher. One short statement. The prototype label stays.
- **Motion moment: the split and the buzz.** On scroll the single watch slides apart into two. A small "Accept" glass button sits between them (the coach accepts each call before it is sent, as the product does). Pressing it, or scrolling past, sends a green pulse outward to both watches; both faces redraw together and both give one long shake. Press again for a changed call and they give two short shakes, which is the product's real buzz pattern.
- **Phone:** the watches stack with the button between them. Where the phone supports vibration, the press also vibrates once, and only on the press.
- **Reduced motion:** two still watches showing the same call, with the buzz pattern in words.
- No claim about speed, range or battery anywhere in this section.

## For programs (new plan)
**Idea:** what a coach and a pitcher see after the game, shown as the pitcher's own page building itself.
- **Layout:** one large glass panel drawn like the portal's pitcher page (pitch mix, usage by count, velocity, what the engine learned), with a two-way switch above it: Coach and Player. The numbers stay as the existing section has them and keep its example labelling.
- **Motion moment: the outing fills in.** As the panel enters, the last outing's pitches drop into the charts one at a time: bars grow pitch by pitch and the count grid fills cell by cell, so the page looks earned by the game above it. Switching between Coach and Player re-lays the same panel with a shared-element move, so the viewer sees what each role gets.
- **Interaction:** hover a pitch type to light it across every chart at once.
- **Phone:** the panel becomes a swipeable set of cards, one chart each, with the switch pinned above.
- **Reduced motion:** the finished panel, with the switch still working.

## Motion summary
Big moments: the film story, the hand-off into the playground, the re-weigh, the at-bat thread, the split and buzz, the outing filling in. Everything else is small and physical: glass that catches the cursor's light, buttons that press, a nav that tightens on scroll, focus rings as finished as hover states. The real cursor stays visible and text stays selectable.

## What the prototype is and is not
- It is a standalone page, not app code: one scrubbed GSAP timeline on Lenis over frames 37 to 72 of the existing film, with the real engine running the story's numbers and the playground.
- Checked by running it: it loads with no page errors at 1440x900 and 390x844; the story's top group is "Fastball, outside" at 42%; the playground controls change the call (for example 3-2 gives "Slider, get it over" at 40% and the face reads SLIDER); refraction renders in Chromium.
- Not checked: Safari, Firefox, a real phone, touch input, frame rate on real hardware (the recordings are headless Chrome at about 25 frames a second, so they show the choreography, not true smoothness). Reduced motion is not built in the prototype. Stops 1 to 3 are not in it.

## Honesty rules that still hold
- Engine-looking numbers come from the real engine. Anything illustrative says so on the page.
- The watch is labelled an illustration and a prototype.
- Not on the page: latency, range or battery claims, device-rule compliance, logos, testimonials, user counts, prices, a waitlist, founder voice.

# PitchIQ site v2: build plan (Nov 16, 2026)

Status: direction chosen by Drew on Nov 16. Nothing is built yet. This plan replaces the "pick a direction" step in `PROMPT_website_build_2026-11-16.md`; everything else in that prompt still applies (content truth, tech, the visual loop, the rules).

Branch: `site/v2-alive` (from `site/watch-redesign`, c6cb854). Mocks: `docs/site-v2-directions/e-film-story.html` (the film story) and `d-floodlight-glass.html` (the glass playground).

## The idea in one line
The watch assembles while the call gets made. As the parts come together, the viewer sees the inputs arrive, then the decision narrow, and it all lands on "Fastball, outside." Then they get to change the situation themselves.

## What Drew decided
- The film stays exactly as built. Only the words and glass over it change.
- During the film, show inputs first, then decisions, building to the final call.
- Premium Apple product-page feel: the product carries one message at a time.
- Glassmorphic, liquid glass.
- Floodlight colors, with a little more green than the color research first allowed.
- Not chosen: Night Game, On the Glass, The Movement (directions A, B, C).

## The look

**Color** (all as CSS variables)
| Name | Value | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `#0c0b0e` | Page background. Same as the film's frames, so there is no seam. |
| Midnight | `#14224f` | Large soft light behind sections |
| Read glow | `#2f5bff` | Inputs: dots, bars, lit controls |
| Chalk | `#ecebe6` | Text |
| Link green | `#62f07a` | The call and what leads to it |

- Blue means "being weighed". Green means "the call".
- Green appears on: the progress rail as it fills, the winning call, the final headline, the rim of the glass panel once the call wins, the "Call landed" chip, the watch rim in the playground, and the primary buttons. Nowhere else.
- Every blue-to-green blend is mixed in OKLab (`#2f5bff` to `#62f07a`), as the color research found it stays in gamut that way.

**Type**
- Hanken Grotesk only, self-hosted. Headlines at weight 650 with tight tracking; body at 400 to 500.
- Headlines are two lines at most. No eyebrow labels, no all-caps labels, no numbered sections.

**Glass**
- Panels: blurred and saturated backdrop, a bright top rim, a soft inner bloom, and a blue glow along the bottom edge that turns green when a call wins.
- Glass is used for four things only: the nav, the film's ledger, the playground panels, and small chips. Text sections sit on the plain ground.
- True lens refraction (bending what is behind the glass) works in Chrome only today. It is a progressive extra to try in the build, not a requirement.

## The page, top to bottom
1. **The film, telling one call** (the six stops below).
2. **Set the situation** (the playground).
3. **The at-bat**: the existing sequence section, restyled. One at-bat, pitch by pitch, each call on a small watch face.
4. **Two wrists**: the pitcher's watch and the catcher's get the same call. Short, with the prototype label.
5. **For programs**: the existing coaches-and-players section, restyled.
6. **Questions** (FAQ, kept).
7. **Talk to us** (contact form, kept and restyled).
8. **Footer.**

The current "Five reads" and "Inside the call" sections go away: the film and the playground now do their job.

## The film: six stops
Layout rule, the same at every stop so the viewer always knows where to look: one statement on the left, the watch in the middle, one glass ledger on the right that fills as they scroll, and a rail at the bottom with three parts (Inputs, Decision, Call).

| Stop | Scroll | Film shows | Statement | Ledger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 to 10% | Parts scattered | Every pitch called. Every call explained. | Empty |
| 2 | 10 to 22% | Case comes in | It starts with the situation. | 2-2 count, one out, runner on third, 3rd inning, tied |
| 3 | 22 to 40% | Board and screen | Then your pitcher. Then the hitter. | Feel for fastball, slider, changeup; same-side hitter; last pitch a slider away |
| 4 | 40 to 62% | Watch closes up | Every pitch, every spot, weighed. | The five likeliest calls with their shares; "He's sitting slider." |
| 5 | 62 to 76% | Screen wakes | Down to one. | The other four fade; the winner turns green |
| 6 | 76 to 100% (rest) | FASTBALL / OUTSIDE | Fastball, outside. | Call landed, the reason, and a "Set the situation" button |

- Ledger rows arrive one at a time with the scroll, not all at once. Older rows dim so the newest one is the bright one.
- Stop 3 needs its statement moved on desktop: the watch is large and off-center in those frames and the text sits on it in the mock.
- The story is one real engine run, computed at build time through the same engine the demo uses, so it cannot drift from the product. In the mock run (engine 1.2.3, sample starter, college tables) fastball down and away is the top call at 26%. The page says "top of the mix", because the engine draws each call from the mix.
- If a later engine version stops putting fastball away on top for this situation, the build must fail loudly so the story gets a new true situation. The film's last frame is fixed, so the words have to match it.
- Fine print at the last stop: what the run was, and that the watch is an illustration and a prototype.

## Set the situation (the playground)
- Left glass panel: the count (big numerals, add a ball, add a strike), the bases (tap to put a runner on), outs, inning and score.
- Middle glass panel: what the engine would throw here, as bars. The top call's bar is the blue-to-green blend. The engine's reason line sits under it.
- Right: the watch face, which redraws with the new call and gets a green rim when it lands.
- It runs the real engine in the browser (the existing demo's data path). The hitter control ships only if it can drive a real engine input; otherwise it is left out, not faked.
- Loads about one screen before it is needed, as the demo does now.

## Motion
Three big moments, and little else:
1. **The film story.** Ledger rows arrive with the scroll, the rail fills blue to green, and at stop 5 the losing calls fall away.
2. **The hand-off.** The last frame holds, the ledger and rail lift away, and the playground's panels rise around the same watch position, so the film reads as the opening of the playground.
3. **The re-weigh.** Change anything in the playground and the bars re-sort, the lines into the watch pulse blue to green, and the face redraws.

Small things: glass that catches light from the cursor, buttons that press, a nav that tightens on scroll, focus rings as finished as the hover states. The real cursor stays visible and text stays selectable.

## Phone (390 wide), designed on purpose
- Film: the statement sits at the top, the watch fills the middle (the tall frames), and the ledger becomes a tray at the bottom showing only the newest rows.
- Playground: the watch comes first, then the controls, then the mix. Controls are thumb-sized and nothing depends on hover.

## Reduced motion
A calm second version, not a broken one: the film is the still last frame, the six stops become a short stacked list (inputs, decision, call) beside it, and the playground updates without animation.

## Honesty rules that still hold
- Engine-looking numbers come from the real engine. Anything illustrative says so on the page.
- The watch is labelled an illustration and a prototype.
- Not on the page: latency, range or battery claims, device-rule compliance, logos, testimonials, user counts, prices, a waitlist, founder voice.

## Not verified yet
- Every mock is static and was shot in Chrome headless only. No motion has been built or tested, and nothing has been checked in Safari or on a real phone.
- Glass with heavy blur can be slow on phones. The mobile Lighthouse target (80 or better) has not been tested against this look.
- No app code has changed, so `tsc`, tests, build, e2e and Lighthouse have not been run for v2.

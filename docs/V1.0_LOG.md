### Two places the plan was wrong, and what was done instead

- **3.6, `GUARD_TYPE` default.** The plan says 0.030. Its own acceptance case for 3.6 — an average changeup to an
  opposite-side hitter at 2-0 surviving the guard — measures a gap of **0.0301**, one ten-thousandth over. Since the
  plan makes this a Phase 5 knob with range [0.020, 0.060], it starts at **0.035**, inside that range, where the
  plan's own test passes.
- **3.6, the sanity check's direction.** The plan says that at `GUARD_TYPE = GUARD_DELTA` the new kept set is a subset
  of v0.9's. It cannot be: any call v0.9 keeps is within `GUARD_DELTA` of the overall best, so its type's best is too
  (type guard passes) and it is within `GUARD_DELTA` of its own type's best (location guard passes). v0.9's set is a
  **subset of the new one**. Equal thresholds can only admit more, which is the point of splitting the guard. The test
  in `tests_v10.py` asserts the true direction, 24/24.

### 3.7 — the mixing operator weighted by real usage

**New files.** `fit_mix_prior.py` reads the 88 Statcast day-files (337,865 pitches, 2024 + 2025 regular season) and
writes `mix_prior.json`:

- `u_T[composition][count][platoon][kind]` — pitch-type usage, the unweighted average of each pitcher's own share in
  that cell. Five compositions clear the 40-pitcher-season gate: `FB+SL+CH` (279), `FB+SL+CB+CH` (210), `FB+SL` (152),
  `FB+SL+CB` (82), `FB+CB+CH` (56). Anything rarer falls back to `u_T_all`, renormalized over the arsenal's kinds.
- `u_L[kind][count][platoon][target]` — target usage from real pitch locations, zones per `statcast_check.zone`.
- Thin cells (under 200 pitches) pool into their count group (ahead / even / behind / 3-0 / full). The pooling is
  resolved at fit time, so both engines do a plain 12-count lookup and cannot disagree.

**Two deviations from the plan's constants, both documented in the script:**

- **`MIN_PITCHES` is 150, not the plan's 300.** The plan's figure was written for a full-season pull. `data/statcast`
  is a 1-in-4-day sample (`fetch_statcast.py` takes every 4th day), so 300 *sampled* pitches is about 1,200 over a
  full season — four times what the plan asked — and it starves three of the five real compositions: at 300 only
  `FB+SL+CB+CH` and `FB+SL+CH` clear the gate. 150 sampled pitches is about 600 full-season pitches, still stricter
  than the plan asked for, and all five clear.
- **A `FLOOR` of 0.005 per cell.** The plan does not mention one, but five of 547 target cells and one pitch-type cell
  came out at exactly zero (all at 3-0, where nobody in the sample threw that pitch to that spot). A hard zero in the
  prior would make the engine unable *ever* to call that spot, and the plan is explicit that only a guard may do that,
  because a guard gives a reason. Every key now keeps at least 0.5% of its cell.

**The operator** (`policy_from_costs`, `app/engine.js` `policyArr`) is the plan's, unchanged:

    c_n      = best_n - lam_L * log SUM_q u_L(q) exp(-(c(n,q) - best_n)/lam_L) taper(., lw)
    pi_n     ~ u_T(n) exp(-(c_n - min_m c_m)/lam_T)
    pi_{q|n} ~ u_L(q) exp(-(c(n,q) - best_n)/lam_L) taper(., lw)

with `KAPPA_T = 3`, `KAPPA_L = 1`. The location window and taper are untouched. The pitch-type stage has no window
any more — 3.6's pitch-type guard does that job and gives a reason when it fires. `mix_prior.json` goes into
`app/engine_data.json` through `app/export_data.py` (its `meta` block is provenance, so it stays out of the bundle and
out of the fingerprint).

**What it bought.** The engine's 0-0 mix now sits on top of real usage, and off-speed is no longer shut out of a
hitter's count (issue 6):

| Situation (D1, FB/SL/CH, grade 3) | engine | real |
|---|---|---|
| 0-0 same side: FB / SL / CH | 65% / 33% / 3% | 61% / 35% / 3% |
| 0-0 opposite: FB / SL / CH | 63% / 20% / 17% | 58% / 24% / 18% |
| 2-0 opposite: CH | 13.9% | 17.1% |
| 0-2 opposite: FB / SL / CH | 39% / 22% / 40% | 44% / 19% / 37% |

The scenario suite went from 49/99 enforced rows passing to **65/99**, still with zero rows over the Not-acceptable
cap. B8b turned green.

**What it did not buy, and why — measured, not guessed.**

- **B6 (middle share) is not an operator problem.** Over the 1,230 CI-grid situations above the B6 limit, the middle's
  cost advantage over the best non-middle call has a median of **0.6 T** and exceeds 3 T in only 1% of them. So the
  middle is not winning by a margin the prior cannot reach; it is winning because the engine's cost model makes the
  middle the cheapest spot for *every* pitch in those situations, and the usage prior only reweights a choice, it
  cannot invent a reason. Two probes confirm the knobs are not the answer: at `KAPPA_L = 3` B6 goes 39.6% → 43.5% while
  B1 (86.6%), B2 (92.5%) and B5 (51.9%) all get worse; with the location window's shrink removed entirely, B6 goes to
  46.3% and B8a gets worse. The middle's *cost* has to come up, which is Phase 4's pitcher-side work.
  Evidence: `results/callsheet_v10_37{,_kl3,_noshrink}.md`.
- **B7 (inside) needs 4a, as the plan's own root-cause table says.** `u_L` puts 32% of same-side 0-0 fastballs inside,
  and the engine still calls inside almost never, because the HBP cost at amateur levels swamps it (root cause R3).
  The HBP fit in 4a is fitted at the real target mix precisely to break that loop.
- **Predictability went up at 2-0, 2-1 and 3-1, not down.** Mean top-call share: 2-1 68.9% → 75.4%, 3-1 77.4% → 87.2%.
  That is the prior working as intended, not a regression: real pitchers in those counts throw the fastball about 70%
  of the time, and the engine now matches them. It does put the prior in tension with band B1 (no pitch type above
  85%, 3-0 exempt), which now holds in 91.4% of eligible situations. Worth a decision in Phase 5: either B1 exempts
  3-1 the way it exempts 3-0, or `KAPPA_T` comes down and the engine is deliberately less like a real staff there.

**The refit, again (R4).** The new mix drifted the level fits, so `fit_all.py` was rerun (114s). The drift is small —
`wo` and `wz` each about +0.04 to +0.08, `hbp` +0.003 to +0.011, `wild` about −0.01 — and all three amateur levels land

`outcome_params.py` is kept in the scratchpad for this session only; `git show HEAD:outcome_params.py` is the durable
copy.

**The MLB realism check, and the refit Drew chose.** The new mix dropped MLB's strikeout rate to **18.9% against
20.0% real** (BB 8.1 vs 7.9), 1.1 points outside the 1-point cap. The cause was measured before anything was changed:
with the prior off the same engine reads 19.7% and passes; with `u_T` alone 19.0%; with both 18.9%. `KAPPA_T` does not
move it at all (18.9% at 1, 2, 3 and 4) and `KAPPA_L` barely (18.9 to 19.0 at 3). In plain terms: **when pitches go
where real MLB pitchers actually put them, the engine's hitter model produces a point fewer strikeouts than reality**,
and the old 19.7% came from concentrating calls on spots that model over-values.

MLB is normally a *check* level -- its shifts were fitted once against MLB pitch-level rates and stored in
`fit_mlb.json`, and `fit_all.py` leaves it alone. Drew's call (Nov 10) was to refit it rather than leave the check red,
so `fit_all.py` gained a documented `--mlb` path: it fits the same three shifts the amateur levels fit (`wild`, `wz`,
`hbp`, with `wo` tied to `wz`), keeps the pitch-level-fitted `cmd`/`zsw`/`osw`/`cs`, leaves the MLB in-play table
alone, and writes the result back into `fit_mlb.json` as well, or the next plain run would revert it.

MLB now lands exactly: **K 20.0%, BB 7.9%, HBP 0.9%**. The shifts that moved: `wz` -0.5611 to -0.4496, `wo` -0.1183 to
-0.0067, `wild` (absent) to -0.0079, `hbp` 0.0305 to 0.0295. The pitch-level rates the original MLB fit was built
against did not get worse -- they got slightly better: zone 49%, z-swing 65%, o-swing 29%, called-strike 14.5% and
first-strike 55% are all unchanged, swinging-strike went 9.0% to 9.7% (real is about 11%) and out-of-zone contact 69%
to 67% (real is about 65%). The pre-refit `fit_mlb.json` is in git history; the amateur levels land on their targets
as before.

The trade-off Drew accepted is worth stating plainly: the MLB level is no longer an *independent* check on the hitter
model, because it has now been fitted at the v1.0 mix. The finding itself -- that the hitter model under-produces
strikeouts at the real target mix -- still belongs in `docs/HANDOFF_hitter_model.md` (Phase 9), because the refit moved
`wz`/`wo`, which are hitter-model keys, to absorb it.

### 3.8 — the at-bat plan

**What it is.** `at_bat_plan(tab)` walks the count chain forward from 0-0 under the policies the table stores, and
returns the chance of reaching each count, the four plate-appearance end chances, the opener, the put-away pitch, the
main calls when behind, and one plain line for the app.

**The one design decision worth recording.** The plan reads `root_call`, not the table's own per-count policy. The
table solves its counts without the myopic guards (that is v0.9 behaviour and 3.7 did not change it), so a plan built
from the table would have been a near-miss of the calls the engine actually makes. Going through `root_call` — both
guards, the sit game re-solved, then the operator — means the plan's opener *is* the live call at 0-0, not a
resemblance of it. The test checks that identity and it holds to the last digit (24.4% and 24.4%).

**The chain.** Every event raises balls or strikes or ends the plate appearance, except a foul with two strikes, which
keeps the count. That makes a two-strike count's own foul share a self-loop, so its reach is the inflow divided by
(1 − that share) — exact, not an iteration — and the counts then resolve in order of balls + strikes. Transitions
match `simulate.py`'s plate-appearance loop event for event, `wp_ball` counted as a ball included.

**Filled on demand, not in `solve_table`.** The pass costs 0.123s against 0.467s for the table solve itself, about
26%. The simulator solves hundreds of thousands of tables and never asks for a plan, so `at_bat_plan` fills and
caches on first use. (It was eager for one revision; `tests.py` went from 13s to over ten minutes, which settled it.)

**Tests** (the plan's three, plus three more): the end chances sum to 1 (largest error 6.66e-16 over 18 plate
appearances); every count's inflow equals what the other counts send it, recomputed independently of the order the
chain resolved them in (exactly 0.00e+00); the reach chances match a 20,000-plate-appearance Monte Carlo over the same
stored policies within 0.01 (largest difference 0.0073); the opener is the call the engine actually makes most often at
0-0; the line reads as one plain sentence; and the put-away pitch really does have the biggest chance-weighted share
across 0-2, 1-2 and 2-2.

**Wired into all three places the plan asks for:**

- `app/parity_v10.js` compares the whole plan — reach chain, end chances, opener, put-away, behind lines and the plan
  line. Which calls, and in what order, must match exactly; the shares match at 1e-9 (largest 6.76e-14).
- `callsheet.py` weights counts by each plate appearance's own reach chain (`wof()`), which is what band B5 was always
  meant to use. B5 went 61.3% to 63.1%; B7's chance-weighted inside share did not move. The Statcast frequency table
  stays as the fallback and as `--weights-uniform`, and missing CSVs are no longer fatal, because the chain supplies
  the weights now.
- The app shows the line under the call, computed off the critical path with the why line, and `app/smoke.js` now
  fails if it is missing or malformed: *"Plan: Slider down and away to start, put away with the Slider down and away."*

**The whole gate is green**, `scripts/test-app.sh` exit 0, with `tests_v10.py` at 43/43.

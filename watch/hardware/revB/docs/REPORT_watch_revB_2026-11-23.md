# PitchIQ custom watch, rev B: engineering report

Date: 2026-11-23. Everything here is design work on a computer. No board has been built, no firmware has run on hardware, and no part has been held or measured.

Research method: `/boss-research-skill` is not installed, so the boss-worker routing skill was used in its place (I planned, briefed and checked; workers researched and reviewed; I re-ran every check myself).

## 1. Executive report

**Status, one label at a time**

| Label | Status | Evidence |

**What rev B is.** A new board for our own compiled firmware. It is not a Bangle.js 2 clone: different pin map, no Espruino, no GPS, compass, barometer or external flash.

| Function | Part | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Processor and Bluetooth | Raytac MDBT50Q-1MV2 module (nRF52840) | Pre-certified radio, chip antenna |
| Power | Nordic nPM1300 | Charger, power path, ship mode, 3.0 V and 1.8 V rails, fuel gauge |
| Always-on display | Sharp LS013B7DH03, 1.28 in 128x128 reflective memory LCD | In stock at DigiKey; frontlight is an option, not a dependency |
| Heart rate | MAX30101 on the wrist side, with a 5 V boost for its green LED | |
| Motion | LIS2DH12 | |
| Vibration | 10 mm coin motor on a transistor | Location in the case is unresolved |
| Button | One side button into the power chip | Wakes from ship mode, hard reset by long press |
| Charge and programming | Six gold pads on the back for a pogo-pin cradle | No USB connector. The +5 V pad is set apart from the other five so a cradle put on backwards cannot put 5 V on a logic pad |

Board: 32 x 36 mm, 6 layers, 0.8 mm thick, parts on both sides. 77 parts, 42 nets, 156 vias. KiCad design-rule check: 0 errors, 0 unconnected, 83 warnings (81 are library-link notices because footprints are generated by script, 2 are silkscreen lines clipped at the battery pads).

**Recommended size: option A (reference size, about 36 x 44 x 11-12 mm case).** It is the only option that uses parts I could confirm in stock and a certified module at a known price. Options B and C are smaller but depend on parts whose price, stock or dimensions I could not confirm (section 5).

**What stops an order today**

1. The display's flex cable may not reach its connector, and after folding its contacts probably face the wrong way for the connector chosen. Needs a display in hand.
2. The 10 mm motor has no place in the stack. Needs a case concept or a smaller motor.
3. The power chip's reference schematic image could not be downloaded (Nordic's site refused every fetch). The circuit follows Nordic's text guidelines and pin tables instead. A person should compare it against the image.
4. KiCad's own electrical rule check was not run (KiCad 7 has no command-line version). A scripted substitute was run. Open the schematic in KiCad and run ERC once.
5. The board was routed by my own autorouter, not by hand and not by a proven tool.

**Buy first (about $160 plus a heart-rate breakout):** one Sharp panel and one Azumo frontlit module, Nordic's nPM1300 evaluation kit, an nRF52840 dev kit. These settle the display cable, the frontlight voltage, the power chip's start-up behaviour and let the firmware run before any board is ordered.

## 3. Software to hardware compatibility

The existing watch app (`pitch-caller/watch/bangle/pitchiq.app.js`) is JavaScript for Espruino and cannot run on this board. Its logic was ported to C.

| Piece | State |
|---|---|
| Portable C core (protocol, crypto, screens, state) | `watch/firmware/core`. Host tests compare it with the original JavaScript: 19 known-answer checks, 231 crypto vectors, 82 scenarios (1,941 steps), all identical. Re-run by me. |
| Zephyr application | `watch/firmware/app`. Compiles for this board and for the nRF52840 dev kit. |
| Phone side | One change needed and **not made**: `app/watch.js` line 335 filters on the name prefix `"Bangle"`. The new firmware advertises as PitchIQ. |
| Pairing | New firmware requires a passkey bond. The phone app's pairing flow has not been tested against it. |

## 6. Architecture

- 3.0 V rail (buck 2): processor, display, accelerometer, logic.
- 1.8 V rail (buck 1): heart-rate sensor logic. Firmware turns it on only when needed.
- 5 V rail (TPS61023 boost from the system rail): heart-rate LED only, fully disconnected when off.
- System rail (3.1-4.2 V on battery, about 5 V in the cradle): motor and boost input.
- One I2C bus: power chip, accelerometer, heart-rate sensor. SPI to the display.
- Battery joins through a solder jumper that is bridged last.

Pin map is in `scripts/design.py` and mirrored in `watch/firmware/boards/pitchiq/pitchiq_watch/`. The firmware reviewer compared the two and found no mismatch.

## 7. Processor analysis

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Raytac MDBT50Q-1MV2 module (chosen)** | Certified radio and antenna, crystal and matching inside, known price ($8.95 seen), works with Zephyr unchanged | 2.05 mm tall, 15.5 mm long, needs a copper-free strip across the board top |
| 8 x 8 mm module (ISP1807) | Much smaller, includes both crystals | Price and stock not retrieved |
| Bare nRF52840 | Smallest and cheapest part | Own antenna design, tuning and full radio certification |
| nRF54L15 | Newer, likely lower power | Power figures not found; not chosen on unverified numbers |

Firmware platform: Zephyr 4.4.2 with MCUboot. Bare-metal was rejected (would mean writing a Bluetooth stack integration and bootloader by hand). For a product sold to others, move to Nordic's nRF Connect SDK so the Bluetooth controller is one with a current qualification.

## 8. Display analysis

Chosen: Sharp LS013B7DH03. Reflective, so it is readable in sunlight with no light and holds an image at about 4 uA. DigiKey showed 33,493 in stock, $12.39 each.

- **Night games.** The bare panel has no light. Option: Azumo's version with a frontlight bonded on (part 11049-06_T7, $32.08 seen at one distributor). The board has pads and a switch for its LED. The LED voltage is not published, so the resistor value is a placeholder until one is measured.
- **Sourcing risk.** Single source. Sharp's distributors quote a 2,240-piece factory minimum and Sharp issued change notices in 2023 and 2024. Fine for prototypes, a real risk for production.
- **Rejected.** Colour memory LCDs seen were obsolete or parallel-interface. E-paper is too slow for a pitch call. OLED and TFT are not always-on at this power.

## 9. Power

`docs/power_budget.md` (estimate; inputs corrected after the power review).

| Use | Battery current | Life on 150 mAh (80% usable) |
|---|---|---|
| Standby, advertising, screen on | 0.06 mA | about 87 days |
| Game, typical (connected, 50 calls an hour, heart rate off) | 0.78 mA | about 6 days of continuous play |
| Game, relaxed link | 0.48 mA | about 10 days of continuous play |
| Game, heavy (heart rate on, frontlight on 30% of the time) | 3.82 mA | about 31 hours |
| One 3-hour game a day plus standby | 3.5 mAh a day | about 34 days (the power reviewer's separate estimate: 27-32 days) |
| Storage in ship mode | 0.37 uA from the power chip | months; the cell's own self-discharge decides. Store at 40-60% and top up every 6 months. |

Peak current is about 150-240 mA when the motor starts. Firmware selects the power chip's high current limit and holds off heart-rate measurement during a buzz. Charging takes about 2.5 hours and stops above 45 C. These numbers need a measurement on the first board.

## 11. Manufacturing plan

- Fabricator: JLCPCB (the only one of those checked that offers 6 layers at 0.8 mm). ENIG gold finish for the cradle pads and the fine-pitch parts.
- Standard (not economic) double-sided assembly, on a panel with rails, fiducials and break-off tabs. The panel drawing is not made.
- Fitted by hand or supplied by us: the module, battery leads, motor leads, display, frontlight tail, battery jumper. The heart-rate sensor is moisture-sensitive and should go on last.
- Check the placement rotations in JLCPCB's preview for every polarised part and all bottom-side parts before paying.
- Files: `out/fab/gerber/` (6 copper layers, mask, paste, drills), `out/fab/cpl_jlcpcb.csv`, `out/fab/bom_jlcpcb.csv`. Regenerate with `scripts/export_fab.sh`.

## 12. Firmware architecture

- `core/`: portable C, no hardware access, tested on a PC against the JavaScript original.
- `app/src/`: Bluetooth service with passkey bonding, display drawing, power chip, sensors, heart-rate estimate, haptics, watchdog.

## 13. Flashing and debug

1. Dev kit first: build for `nrf52840dk/nrf52840`, `west flash`. This needs no custom board.
2. Custom board: a debug probe on the cradle pads (SWDIO, SWDCLK, nRESET, GND, and the 3.0 V pad as the probe's voltage reference). The probe must use 3.0 V logic. Always have a battery attached when programming (section 16, risk 3).
3. After the first flash, updates go over Bluetooth with the mcumgr tool.
4. Logs: Segger RTT through the same probe.

Commands are in `watch/firmware/README.md`.

## 14. Migration plan

1. Run the firmware on the dev kit with breakout boards for the display, heart-rate sensor and accelerometer.
2. Change the phone app's name filter and test pairing with the passkey flow.
3. Run the power chip on Nordic's evaluation kit with the real battery and the same resistor settings.
4. Order boards only after the section 19 list is clear.
5. Keep the Bangle.js version working until a custom watch has passed the test plan; nothing in this work changes it.

## 15. Assembly and bring-up

Full 19-step checklist: `reviews/qa.md` section 7. The order that matters:

1. Inspect, then measure for shorts on every rail before applying power.
2. Power the power chip **alone** (module and display not fitted) from a current-limited 5 V supply. Confirm 3.0 V and 1.8 V. Do this with a battery and without.
3. Measure the battery pad polarity before bridging the battery jumper. There is no reverse-polarity protection.
4. Fit the module, flash, check current in sleep.
5. Display, then accelerometer, motor, heart rate, charging, Bluetooth range.

## 16. Risk register

| # | Risk | Severity | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Display flex cannot reach the connector within Sharp's bend rules; contacts face the wrong way after folding | Blocker | Open. Needs a panel in hand; may need a top-contact connector and a moved connector. |
| 2 | No location for the 10 mm motor | Blocker | Open. Use an 8 mm motor or a case bay. |
| 3 | Power chip start-up from the cradle with no battery: other users report the 3.0 V rail stuck low or as high as 4.2-4.45 V, above the processor's 3.9 V limit | Major | Mitigated by procedure (bring-up step 2, program with a battery attached). Not proven. |
| 4 | Power chip circuit not compared with Nordic's reference schematic image | Major | Open. |
| 5 | Autorouted board, never reviewed by a layout engineer; buck and boost loops are longer than Nordic's reference layout | Major | Open. Review found no clear fault at these currents. |
| 6 | Module's FCC grant assumes 5 mm between antenna and body; a watch may not meet that | Major | Open. Ask Raytac or a test lab before selling. |
| 7 | Antenna performance on the wrist next to a battery and display | Major | Untested. Copper-free strip is full board width; display and battery outlines sit below it. |
| 8 | Over-discharged cell may not recharge; cell protection thresholds unpublished | Major | Open. Bench test; buy a cell with documented protection for production. |
| 9 | Boost inductor has no published saturation current | Minor | Open. Measure start-up current on the first board. |
| 10 | Frontlight LED voltage unknown | Minor | Open until a module is measured. |
| 11 | Heart-rate accuracy during play | Major for that feature | Untested; the estimate algorithm has never seen real data. |
| 12 | Display single-sourced with a large factory minimum | Major for production | Open. |
| 13 | Cradle pads touch skin | Minor | Series resistors and clamp diodes added; gold finish; case should expose as little as possible. |
| 14 | A via of the same net can sit with its copper 0.05 mm from the pad it serves (0.15 mm was not routable around the power chip); vias of other nets are at least 0.2 mm from any pad. Four vias are inside the power chip's centre pad | Minor | Accepted for prototypes; ask the assembler. |
| 15 | If the 32.768 kHz crystal fails to start, the firmware waits for it forever | Minor | Open. No fallback to the internal oscillator is configured. |
| 16 | The watchdog starts before the first feed; a stuck I2C bus at boot could exceed its 8 s and cause a reset loop. The fatal-error handler has no reboot-loop guard | Minor | Open (found in the second review, not fixed). |
| 17 | With one bond slot, a phone that forgets the watch cannot pair again until the bond is cleared with the button hold | Minor | Open; document it for users. |
| 18 | The 5 V clamp diode on the charge pad sits at its rated standoff when docked (5.0-5.25 V) | Minor | Open. Check its leakage in the datasheet or change to a higher-voltage clamp. |

## 20. Final review

Eight independent reviews were run on the first rev B package: electrical, firmware, RF, power, PCB manufacturing, mechanical, sourcing and QA. Full reports are in `reviews/`. No reviewer could build or measure anything.

| Review | Worst finding | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical | Cradle start-up risk; no voltage-reference pad; unprotected pads | Sixth pad (3.0 V reference), 100 ohm series resistors and clamp diodes on the three logic pads, unused pins grounded, extra bypass capacitor, bring-up procedure |
| Firmware | Bonds were lost at every reset (blocker) | Persistent storage enabled; watchdog; authenticated characteristics; advertising restart; start-up race removed; I2C retries |
| RF | Copper-free strip too narrow | Strip now full board width; display and battery outlines moved down; transmit power +8 dBm; longer supervision timeout |
| Power | Charger temperature window too wide for the cell; budget errors | Charging stops at 45 C; input limit raised when docked; pull-downs changed to 1 M; budget corrected |
| Manufacturing | Vias touching pads | Vias kept 0.05 mm clear of pads (0.15 mm was not routable); smaller drill; tighter mask |
| Mechanical | Display cable, connector side, motor location (three blockers) | Mounting holes, thermistor moved under the cell. **Blockers remain open.** |
| Sourcing | Wrong prices; frontlight part was only the film | Prices and frontlight part corrected |
| QA | The check proves consistency, not correctness | Stated here; bring-up checklist written |

**Second review, after the fixes.** A fresh reviewer re-read the source files and measured the board. Of the first-round blocker and major findings it counted about 18 fixed on paper, 8 partly fixed, 6 that need hardware and 14 still open (the open ones are in the risk register and the checklist; most cannot be closed in files). It confirmed the new protection parts are wired on the correct side, no copper in the antenna strip on any layer, the firmware fixes are present in the code, the power-chip register values match Zephyr's own driver, and the pin map still matches.

It found one new major fault that my fix had introduced: with all six pads in one row, a cradle reversed end-for-end would have put 5 V on the 3.0 V pad. I moved the +5 V pad out of the row, re-routed, and re-ran the full check (output below). That last change has not been reviewed by anyone else. Its minor findings are risks 14-18; they are not fixed.

**What the automated check does and does not prove.** `scripts/check.sh` proves the schematic, board, parts list and firmware pin map all agree with one source file, and that the board passes KiCad's design rules. It cannot find an error in that source file. If a pin number in `design.py` is wrong, every check still passes.

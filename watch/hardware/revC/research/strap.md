## 2. Silicone vs FKM for this use

Source quality warning: most comparative claims below come from sellers' blogs (RobustGoods, CNS Watch Bands) or an unreferenced forum post, not tests. Treat directionally. Hard numbers are flagged.

| UV | CNS rates "Good", degrades faster in sustained UV; RobustGoods says fades/brittle sooner | CNS rates "Excellent" | same two |
| Sunscreen | NOT FOUND in any fetched source | NOT FOUND | (Suunto care page failed to fetch) |
| Lint/dust | "lint and dust magnet" (BladeForums post); "more prone to attracting lint" (RobustGoods) | said not to collect lint | https://www.bladeforums.com/threads/watch-straps-silicone-fkm-or-vulcanized-rubber-a-technical-study-of-what-we-buy.2082240/ |

| Cleaning | Easy to rinse but attracts dust (RobustGoods) | May need occasional mild soap (RobustGoods) | RobustGoods |
| Price (as seen) | $19.99 Archer, $21.74 Barton | $48.74 Barton (sold out), $75 Crafter Blue (sold out), GBP 51.95 UX07 | section 1 |
| Seller lifespan claims | RobustGoods 6-24 months; CNS 2-4 yr hard use | RobustGoods 3-5 yr; CNS 5+ yr | seller claims only |

Take-away (ESTIMATE, judgement from the above): for sunscreen-and-sweat sun exposure, FKM is probably the better material but costs 2-3.5x and was out of stock at both Barton and Strapcode on the fetch date. Silicone is the cheaper way to prototype; its weak point is tear at the hole, which a well-moulded strap with a reinforced bar pocket and 70A-ish compound handles acceptably (Barton and Archer both ship QR bars integrated). Note that "TPSiV" (Watch Straps AU) is a thermoplastic silicone vulcanizate, not conventional LSR, so the Wacker figures are not applicable to it.

### Sourced numbers
- Spring bar body diameters: 1.8 mm standard "fits most", 1.5 mm thin, 2.0 mm thick, 2.5 mm extra thick (Seiko) - https://thelostspringbar.substack.com/p/a-practical-guide-to-spring-bars . CNS lists ~1.8 standard, 1.75 quick-release, 1.45 slim quick-release, 2.0+ fat - https://cnswatchbands.com/blog/spring-bar-guide/ . Esslinger lists 1.8/1.5/1.3/1.2/1.0 double-flange etc. - https://blog.esslinger.com/watch-band-pin-illustrations/
- Tip (pivot) diameters: Archer help page: body 1.8 mm, pins "about 0.9 mm" - https://help.archerwatchstraps.com/article/267-spring-bar-dimensions ; Omega Forums post: "industry standard 0.9 mm tips", 17 mm bars - https://omegaforums.net/threads/jlc-reverso-classique-spring-bar-size-lug-hole-damage.170363/ ; Watch Straps AU quick-release: 1.5 mm body, 1.0 mm tip, 1.5 mm tip length - A4 above.
- ISO 3765:1998 "Wristwatches - Dimensions of bracelet-to-case fastening elements" - https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/c22ac31a-2209-4fad-b2aa-cf37b10ef4d2/iso-3765-1998 and sample PDF https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/9269/6f61282f001547608912040e8284e89a/ISO-3765-1998.pdf (the extracted table text is garbled; column mapping partly inferred by the reader, verify against the purchased standard):
  - lug distance A listed incl. 18, 19, 20, 22, 24 mm; tolerance class H13
  - hole in lug D1 = pin diameter d1 + 0.10 mm (or body d + 0.05); tolerance H11
  - useful hole depth L1 = pivot length l2 + 0.05; hole length L2 = l2 + 0.10
  - pin d1 values 0.56 / 0.75 / 0.80 / 0.90 / 1.00 / 1.20; body d2 1.0-2.5 mm; pivot l2 0.7-1.2 mm (as extracted)
- Bar length: must equal lug gap ("a 20 mm watch needs 20 mm spring bars", CNS). Bar length is the overall length incl. both pins is NOT stated on any page; I treat nominal = lug gap, which is the retail convention (ESTIMATE of definition).
- Quick-release correctly fitted is described as equal in security to standard (CNS, seller claim). Quick-release strap lug gap must exactly match strap width (Barton A2 page).
- Lug-hole depth recommendation, wall around hole, lever clearance, centre-to-case distance, plastic vs metal lug thickness: NOT FOUND on any fetched source. The numbers below are ESTIMATES.
- Printing/moulding limits (sourced): MJF nylon min wall 0.5 mm, min hole 0.5 mm, typical tolerance +/-0.25 mm (or +/-0.1% whichever greater - read as 0.025 mm per 25.4 mm), min feature 0.5 mm - https://www.stratasys.com/siteassets/sdm/content---website-storage/design-guides/dg_mjf_stratasys_direct_03062023.pdf (page does not name PA12). ## 4. Retention under activity (ESTIMATE / engineering judgement unless a source is named)

| Attachment | Failure modes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Quick-release spring bar (strap-integrated) | Lever snags and pops one end; tip wears plastic hole, so the bar walks out; dirt in hole; worn tip after many swaps; wrong gap | Used by Archer, Barton, Timex, Watch Straps AU. CNS (seller) says equal security when fitted correctly. Check engagement on both sides. |
| Standard double-flange bar | Needs a tool; bar can bend or slip if tip is short; strap tears at bar hole under tension (reported for silicone, BladeForums) | Cheapest and most standard (1.8 mm per sources) |
| Screw bar (Esslinger lists 1.4-2.0 mm, 23-26.5 mm long) | Screw loosens under vibration without thread-lock; needs through-holes and a driver | Strongest mechanically; slowest to swap |
| Integrated / pass-through (NATO-style, moulded-in lug) | Pass-through puts the watch back on the strap; moulded-in means the whole case/strap interface is custom and costs tooling | Eliminates the spring bar failure but needs custom parts (see 1e tooling) |

Safest low-cost choice [E]: a standard 1.5-1.8 mm solid or screw bar through plastic lugs with a through-hole, using a strap with a reinforced bar pocket, or quick-release bars with the lever recessed so it cannot be hit. For prototypes use quick-release bars because swapping strap lengths is fast; before the pilot, run a 4-hour wear/impact test (arm swings, sweat, bat contact) and decide. This is a judgement; no source in my fetches compares retention rates.

**18 mm or 20 mm for a 34-36 mm wide case?** Sources: a 2:1 case-to-strap ratio is cited with 36 mm / 18 mm and 38 mm / 19 mm examples, and a commenter lists 41/20, 39/20, 39/19, 38/20, 42.5/22 as "pretty much 2:1" - https://www.watchcrunch.com/Chinshandcraft/posts/do-you-know-the-golden-ratio-of-2-1-of-case-size-to-lug-width-244835 . Analog Shift gives 34 mm vintage Rolex with 19 mm lugs and many modern 40 mm watches with 20 mm, but states no ratio - https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/a-guide-to-watch-sizing . Arithmetic: 18/34-36 = 0.50-0.53; 20/34-36 = 0.56-0.59. So 18 mm is the "textbook" proportion, and 20 mm is slightly strap-heavy for the width. Counter-point [E]: your case is 40-44 mm long and 10-12 mm thick, so it wears like a larger, sporty watch, and the 20 mm range has more retail and FKM options (Barton FKM and Crafter Blue are 20/22 only in the pages I read). Recommendation: **20 mm** for supply and retention (wider strap spreads load; more FKM choice), with the lug design able to accept 18 mm via a spacer test on a prototype. If appearance proportions are the priority, choose 18 mm. This is a judgement, not a sourced result.
